Lyrics on your Mac desktop, six ways

LWBy Luz Wang·Founder, Terse·Updated Oct 9, 2026

Apple Music and Spotify keep their lyrics inside their own windows. If you want the words on the desktop itself — floating, in the menu bar, or built into the wallpaper — here are the six ways that work on a Mac in 2026, with what each one is bad at.

Inside the player, on top of the desktop, or in it

The six split into three groups. Inside the player — Apple Music and Spotify — costs nothing extra and is always in sync, but the lyrics vanish the moment the player's window is covered. On top of the desktop — LyricsX, Lyric Fever's karaoke popup, DesktopLyrics — keeps a line visible while you work, as a separate window or menu-bar item. In the desktop is what Terse does: the wallpaper itself rebuilds each line out of particles, so the lyrics share the screen with your windows instead of sitting on top of them.

A note on timing that applies to all of them: synced lyrics are almost always LRC files, timed per line. A highlight that sweeps across a line is an interpolation over that line's length — close, but not word-by-word.

The six, one by one

Apple Music (built in) Inside the Music app Included with an Apple Music subscription

Works with. Apple Music catalog songs

Strength. Time-synced lyrics with nothing to install: click the Lyrics button, or open Window › Full Screen Player and follow along.

Limits. Only inside the Music window, and only for songs from the Apple Music catalog — Apple greys the Lyrics button out for anything else.

Pick it when: You only listen in Apple Music and don't need lyrics outside its window.

Spotify (built in) Inside the Spotify app Free and Premium

Works with. Spotify

Strength. Lyrics scroll in real time inside the app, for free and Premium listeners alike.

Limits. Only inside the Spotify window, and availability varies by song and market.

Pick it when: You listen on Spotify and are happy reading lyrics in its own window.

LyricsX Desktop overlay + menu bar Free, open source (MPL-2.0)

Works with. Most Mac music players

Strength. The long-standing open-source choice: searches several lyrics sources automatically, floats the current line on the desktop and in the menu bar, and lets you set the font, colour and position. brew install --cask lyricsx.

Limits. The last tagged release is v1.6.3 from April 2022, although the repository still receives commits.

Pick it when: You want a free, customisable floating lyric line and don't mind an older release.

Lyric Fever Menu bar, full screen, karaoke popup Free, open source (MIT)

Works with. Spotify desktop and Apple Music

Strength. Actively maintained (v3.3, November 2025). Lyrics come from Spotify, then LRCLIB, then NetEase; Apple's on-device translation and offline caching are built in. brew install lyric-fever.

Limits. Spotify and Apple Music only, and it needs macOS Sequoia or later.

Pick it when: You use Spotify or Apple Music on a current macOS and want a polished, free lyrics app.

DesktopLyrics Desktop text Free

Works with. Apple Music app

Strength. Simple and free (v2.7.2, December 2024; macOS 10.15+). Font, size, colour and shadow are adjustable for the lyrics and the song info.

Limits. It shows lyrics already embedded in your music files — it does not fetch any — and long lyrics are split into pages.

Pick it when: Your library already has lyrics embedded and you play it in the Music app.

Terse The wallpaper itself Music mode free · Pro $4.99/mo

Works with. Anything that reports to Now Playing (Apple Music, Spotify, NetEase Cloud Music, most players)

Strength. The lyrics are the wallpaper: every line is built from particles and swept as it is sung, next to your notifications and the rest of the live wallpaper. Lyrics come from a .lrc file, the song file's own tags, LRCLIB, then NetEase, and are cached locally. No microphone, nothing recorded.

Limits. It is a whole live-wallpaper app, heavier than a menu-bar utility. Lyrics are line-timed, not per-word karaoke, and they sit behind your windows unless Pro floats them on top.

Pick it when: You want lyrics to be part of your desktop rather than a box on top of it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I show song lyrics on my Mac desktop?

Apple Music and Spotify show synced lyrics inside their own windows. To see lyrics on the desktop itself, use a separate app: LyricsX floats the current line on the desktop and in the menu bar, Lyric Fever adds a menu-bar line and a karaoke popup for Spotify and Apple Music, DesktopLyrics shows embedded lyrics on the desktop, and Terse builds each line into the wallpaper as particles.

Can I see Spotify lyrics outside the Spotify window on a Mac?

Yes. Lyric Fever shows Spotify lyrics in the menu bar, full screen and in a karaoke popup; LyricsX floats them on the desktop; and Terse reads the song from Now Playing and draws the lyrics on the wallpaper.

Are there free desktop lyrics apps for Mac?

Yes. LyricsX (MPL-2.0) and Lyric Fever (MIT) are free and open source, DesktopLyrics is free, and Terse's music mode is free — Pro adds floating lyrics above all windows and 15 appearance controls.

Why don't desktop lyrics highlight word by word?

Most synced lyrics are LRC files, which are timed per line, not per word. Apps that sweep a highlight across a line are interpolating over that line's duration. Terse does this and says so; it is close to the music but it is not true per-word timing.

Which lyrics app works with any music player?

Apps that read the system's Now Playing information can follow most players. Terse works that way, so Apple Music, Spotify, NetEase Cloud Music and most other players are recognised. Lyric Fever targets Spotify and Apple Music, and DesktopLyrics targets the Music app.

Does Terse record audio to recognise songs?

No. Terse reads the Now Playing metadata your player already publishes — title, artist, album, length and position. It never uses the microphone or captures audio.
